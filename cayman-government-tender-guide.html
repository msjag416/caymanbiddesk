How to respond to a Cayman Islands Government tender

Where Cayman government opportunities are advertised, what the KY$100,000 threshold means, what bidders need in place before a notice opens, and how responses are scored.

Published
4 November 2026
Last reviewed
4 November 2026
Applies to
Suppliers bidding for Cayman Islands Government, statutory authority and government company contracts
Law
Procurement Act (2023 Revision) and Procurement Regulations (2022 Revision)
Sources
Central Procurement Office, for suppliers · Government support centre · CPO debriefing guidance

Where Cayman government tenders are advertised

The Cayman Islands Government publishes its bidding opportunities on Bonfire, its e-procurement system, at cayman.bonfirehub.com. The Central Procurement Office calls this the Public Purchasing Portal. Some opportunities are also advertised in local newspapers, and in that case the advert or bid document explains how to respond.

The CPO also publishes procurement plans, which show what government entities expect to buy. Reading them lets a business prepare before a notice goes live rather than starting on the day it opens.

The KY$100,000 procurement threshold

Government entities, statutory authorities and government companies must post projects valued at KY$100,000 or more on Bonfire. Smaller projects may appear there too, but only if the entity chooses to post them. For other small value work, the government's guidance is to contact the procuring entity directly and ask to be included.

Value also shapes how a procurement is run and who approves it. The CPO notes that a process may be overseen by an Entity Procurement Committee or by the Public Procurement Committee. At a June 2026 CPO seminar, officials said a project of KY$250,000 and above can take six to eight months from start to finish, while a project around KY$60,000 can take a few weeks.

Register on the portal before you need it

Supplier registration on Bonfire is free. You need to register and log in to download a project's supporting documents, which set out the qualification requirements and scope of work. Registering early matters, because a first-time registration on the closing day is where submissions get lost.

Projects open and close frequently, so it's worth checking the portal on a regular schedule or setting up notifications for your categories.

What eligibility documents to have ready

A valid trade and business licence from the Department of Commerce and Investment is a standard requirement. Depending on the contract, a bid may also ask for child protection training certificates and evidence of insurance or pension coverage. CPO guidance also points to the Licensing, Labour and Pensions Acts and the Procurement Act and Regulations as useful background for bidders.

Keep these documents current and in one place. An expired certificate found the night before the deadline can remove an otherwise strong bid from consideration.

Each tender document states how every section is weighted. Read the weightings first, then decide where your writing time goes.

How a Cayman government bid is scored

Bids are evaluated against the criteria and weightings disclosed in the bid document. A response usually has three parts: eligibility, technical and pricing. The technical part is where you show how you'll deliver the work, and it can include warranties or specification documents. CPO officials have said that value for money means more than the lowest price, with capability and quality also counted.

A compliance matrix helps here. It lists every requirement in the tender beside the page of your response that answers it, so nothing is missed and the evaluator can find each answer quickly.

Asking clarification questions

During the bidding period, questions go through the portal or to the contact named in the bid document. Ask early. Answers may be shared with every bidder, and they can change how you price or structure your response.

Debriefing after an unsuccessful bid

Under the Procurement Regulations, an entity must provide debriefing information to any unsuccessful bidder that asks for it. The CPO says debriefings happen after contract award and within 30 days of the supplier's request, in writing, by phone, by video or in person. A debrief explains how your bid scored against the evaluation criteria. It doesn't compare your bid with other submissions, and it isn't an appeal. A complaint about how a procurement was run goes through the procuring entity's own complaints process.

A tender response checklist

  1. Register on Bonfire and confirm you can download bid documents.
  2. Check your trade and business licence and any certificates the notice asks for.
  3. Note the closing date and time, and set your internal deadline at least two working days earlier.
  4. List every requirement in a compliance matrix and assign an owner to each.
  5. Send clarification questions early, through the portal or the named contact.
  6. Draft the technical response against the published weightings.
  7. Review pricing against the scope and any required format.
  8. Check every requirement in the matrix is answered, then submit before the deadline.
