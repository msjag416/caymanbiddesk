Where Cayman opportunities are published

Where to find Cayman government tenders, and how they work.

The Cayman Islands Government runs a competitive bid process governed by the Procurement Act (2023 Revision), the Procurement Regulations (2022 Revision), the Freedom of Information Act and Central Procurement Office policy. Government is required to advertise electronically where the expected value of a purchase is KY$100,000 or greater, which means opportunities above that threshold appear on the official portal. Purchases below it may never be advertised publicly at all.

Suppliers register once on the portal and then respond to individual notices there. Registration ahead of a deadline matters, because a first-time registration on the closing day is where submissions get lost.

CIMA regulatory updates for Cayman financial services

Factual summaries of published regulatory measures, each linked to the issuing authority. Our commentary is marked separately from source information.

In force since 18 Sep 2026
CIMA · Published 20 July 2026

Rule on Effective Compliance Programme for the Prevention and Detection of ML, TF and PF

Regulator
Cayman Islands Monetary Authority
Effective
18 September 2026
Applies to
CIMA registered and licensed financial services providers

The rule sets binding minimum standards for an AML/CFT/CPF compliance programme, including a documented governance framework, defined roles and lines of accountability, and a documented risk based approach. Requirements previously addressed through guidance become enforceable obligations.

Cayman Bid & Compliance Services commentary: requirement mapping and the organisation of implementation evidence can involve substantial documentation work, and both are separable from the regulatory interpretation that belongs to your advisers.

CIMA · Published 20 July 2026

Rule on Compliance with Financial Sanctions and Targeted Financial Sanctions

Regulator
Cayman Islands Monetary Authority
Effective
18 September 2026
Applies to
CIMA registered and licensed financial services providers

Published alongside the AML rule following industry consultation, this rule sets binding requirements for sanctions and targeted financial sanctions compliance, including screening obligations. Both rules took effect 60 days after publication in the Gazette.

Cayman Bid & Compliance Services commentary: where sanctions screening records sit across more than one system, consolidating them into a single reviewable package is a documentation exercise worth completing now that the rule is in force.
